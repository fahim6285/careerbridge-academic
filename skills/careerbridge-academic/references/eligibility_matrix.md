# Eligibility and role-fit evaluation

## Classification
- **Mandatory:** explicit degree/field/equivalence, appointment grade, years or type of experience, language, disciplinary licensing/accreditation, work authorization or other stated hard rules.
- **Preferred:** publications in a field, teaching domains, grants, mentorship, collaborations, editorial service, leadership or accreditation experience when advertisement labels them desirable.
- **Unknown:** no source fact confirms eligibility; do not convert 'unknown' into 'yes'.
- **Potentially conditional:** licensing, country-specific degree recognition, attestation, immigration; verify relevant official authority and employer policies.

## Matching rubric
For each item copy a *short paraphrase* of the employer's criterion, link to the exact posting/section, identify candidate evidence with a source (e.g. verified CV section, degree certificate, employment certificate), and set `Meets`, `Partial`, `Not met`, `Unknown`, or `Not applicable`. Put documentary evidence and corrective action in separate fields. 'Partial' or 'Not met' must not be concealed; highlight adjacent strengths without misrepresenting an essential criterion.

## Degree recognition and local policy
Distinguish regulator/institution/professional-body criteria and the temporal version in force. An assumption that one country's academic rank automatically transfers to another is invalid. Do not determine final eligibility when an employer retains discretionary authority; describe factual fit and need for employer confirmation.

## Candidate positioning
Write a brief role-fit synopsis: two or three verifiable high-relevance strengths, gaps or conditions and prioritized next steps. Do not automatically recommend submitting to roles with clearly unmet non-waivable criteria; don't invent waiver policies.
